A fast, bit-exact Slay the Spire 2 engine in Rust, and an AI that plays it

spire-mentor reimplements Slay the Spire 2 from scratch, checks itself against the game's own code after every action, and runs fast enough to search thousands of futures per decision, with a run-level AI on top. Two front ends show that AI thinking: a browser workbench and a mod for the real game.

Overview

Six pieces, each usable on its own:

Engine

Rust, deterministic, the whole game: five characters, ~580 cards, ~300 relics, three acts. Builds and tests with nothing else installed.

Parity harness

Runs the game's own assembly headless and checks the engine against it after every single action.

Run AI

Chance-node MCTS for fights; a PPO-trained transformer per character for every other decision.

Workbench

The engine in WebAssembly with the game's look: play, rewind, branch, ask the AI, review.

Spire Mentor

A mod that puts the AI inside the real game as a coach, an autopilot and a rewind button.

Extraction & wiki

just import-game decompiles your copy and extracts data, art and sound; a generator builds a static wiki.

The engine

sts2-rs re-implements the game's rules in Rust, pinned to game build v0.107.1. It is not an approximation: the random number generator is a hand port of .NET's System.Random (the 56-element subtractive generator the game uses), and every RNG stream is consumed in the game's order. A seed yields the same map, the same card rewards, the same shuffles and the same monster rolls as the real game.

The 7,000× is not "Rust vs C#": it is a tight engine versus the game's asynchronous, presentation-driven action pipeline. Numbers are from the project's own benchmarks. The search timings compare a 2.1 GHz Xeon (native, and Chrome on the same box) with a current Android tablet; in the browser the search runs in a Web Worker, and small searches pay a fixed setup cost that larger ones amortise.

Workspace

A cargo workspace of twelve crates: sts2-core (state, hooks, damage, powers, RNG), sts2-content (every card, relic, potion, monster and event), sts2-mcts, sts2-runai (run arena, seed panels, policy/value nets with ONNX inference), sts2-cli (REPL, replays, sweeps), sts2-wire, sts2-py (PyO3 bindings), sts2-bench, sts2-workbench + sts2-workbench-wasm, and helpers. 7,000+ Rust tests, plus a cargo-fuzz target on the combat step.

Parity with the game

Claims of accuracy are cheap, so the project checks them mechanically. The game's own C# assembly, taken from your copy, is IL-patched and run headless with Godot stubbed out. The engine and that oracle then play the same seed with the same actions, in lockstep:

  • Zero combat divergences on all five characters, on fuzzed seeds covering every card (base and upgraded), every card against every arena, pairwise hook interactions and 30-turn fights; the full tier runs 5,000 seeds.
  • Whole-run parity 250/250 seeds across all five characters: map generation, event pools, rewards, shops and the run state carried between acts.
  • Freshness gates refuse to compare against a stale engine build or a stale oracle: a source hash is compiled into the Python extension and checked at load.
  • The browser build is held to the same standard: 46 recorded scenarios, 8,454 requests, zero divergences between the WASM workbench and the Python one.

Details: parity-suite.md.

The AI

Fights: chance-node MCTS

Every combat decision is an MCTS search over the engine: 8,000 simulations per decision in the real game and the published win rates, 1,000 (4,000 at bosses) in training gates. Draws and monster rolls are chance nodes, not clairvoyance; leaves are scored by a 216-feature evaluation.

Everything else: PPO

Paths, card rewards, removals, upgrades, transforms, enchantments, events, shops, rest sites and relic choices come from a transformer policy per character, trained with PPO by self-play over full runs in the simulator and served from Rust through ONNX.

Read more: How the AI plays (search, hidden information, the network) and How it's trained (PPO, compute, the promotion gate, what didn't work).

One fresh seed panel for all five champions, measured October 2026: 200 runs per cell at A0–A8 and 500 at A9–A10, the same seeds for every character, Act 1 rolled between Overgrowth and Underdocks. Combat search is honest (it does not see the draw pile order) at 8,000 simulations per decision, boss fights included, the advisor's budget. A cell's 95% interval is roughly ±6 pp in the middle of the range and ±2–4 pp at A9–A10. The cliff lines up with the game's own steps: A9 (Deadly Enemies) makes every enemy hit harder, and A10 (Double Boss) adds a second boss fight at the end of the run. Full write-up: docs/experiments/2026-10-11-release-winrates.md.

Every champion is promoted on a pre-registered gate: 200 seeds × 11 ascension levels = 2,200 paired runs against the incumbent, McNemar test, seed-clustered error check, and a guard that no single level may regress by more than 5 pp. Promotion records and provenance live next to the nets in sts2-pycli/data/ppo/.

Browser workbench

The engine compiled to WebAssembly, running in a Web Worker behind a React UI styled after the game. No server: saves live in IndexedDB and the site installs as an offline app. Without the game it runs with the real card text and placeholder art; just import-game fills in the real art and sound from your copy.

What you can do

  • Play any character, seed and ascension yourself, or let the PPO champion play a whole run while you watch, then take over at any point.
  • Rewind and branch any decision; the run tree keeps every fork, with named saves.
  • Ask the AI: combat hint, the full MCTS search tree, rankings for card rewards, shops, events and rest sites, "play best".
  • Review a finished run: regret per decision and win-probability-added over the whole run.
  • Lab tools: replay viewer, MCTS-runs browser, deck analysis, SimLab (a sandbox for building positions and running seed-paired strategy studies), and state import: paste a position copied from the live game and analyse it in the browser.

Run it: just workbench-local builds the site into sts2-pycli/web/dist and serves it on http://127.0.0.1:4173. With real art the build contains the game's images and sound, extracted from your copy for your own use, so please run it locally and don't host it publicly. Architecture: browser-workbench.md.

Spire Mentor: the AI inside the real game

A C# mod for Slay the Spire 2 that connects the running game to the AI stack over a local TCP socket. Installed but idle, it leaves the game vanilla. The advisor backend is launched by the mod and ships as a self-contained executable.

Advisor

You play. Ranked moves with MCTS statistics and the principal line, card-reward and shop scores, map routes, a win estimate.

AI turns & autopilot

Let the AI take one decision or play until you stop it; autopilot plays whole runs unattended and never falls back to random play.

Rewind

Every room is snapshotted with its RNG state. Undo a turn, restart a room, or go back to any earlier room, bit-exact.

Workbench link

Open the current state in the workbench, browse the live game's tree, and rewind the real game from the page.

Install

Windows, the game at v0.107.1, the .NET 9 SDK and BaseLib in the game's mods/ folder. Then cd spire-mentor-mod && dotnet build builds the mod and installs it into mods/SpireMentor/. Configuration, the console commands and the wire protocol are in spire-mentor-mod/README.md and PROTOCOL.md.

Getting started

Three tiers; each needs a little more than the last.

1 · Engine only

No game needed. Rust 1.96, cargo-nextest, just 1.38+.

just rs-check
just rs-test        # cargo nextest run --workspace
just rs-doc-test

2 · Parity oracle

Adds your game install, the .NET 9 SDK and uv.

just import-game "/path/to/Slay the Spire 2" --tier oracle
cd sts2-pycli && uv sync --dev
just build-cs && uv run pytest tests

3 · Workbench with real art

Adds ilspycmd, Node 20+, the wasm32-unknown-unknown target and wasm-bindgen-cli 0.2.120.

just import-game "/path/to/Slay the Spire 2"
just workbench-local   # → http://127.0.0.1:4173

Use cargo nextest, never bare cargo test: tests rely on per-process isolation. Full prerequisites: bring-your-own-game.md; Windows: the engine tier runs from PowerShell, the rest under WSL2 (BUILDING-WINDOWS.md).
